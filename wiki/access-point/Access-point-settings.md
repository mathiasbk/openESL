# Access point settings

Most settings can be changed while the AP runs: on the **Settings** page of the [dashboard](Dashboard), through the API, or with [esl-client](esl-client). A few are set when the firmware is built.

## While it runs

| Setting | How |
|---|---|
| Default poll interval for all labels | `esl_client.py config --poll-s 60`, or `PUT /api/config {"poll_s": 60}`. `0` lets each label keep its own. |
| Poll interval for one label | `PUT /api/tags/{mac}/config {"poll_s": 300}` |
| How a label waits when the AP is gone | `PUT /api/tags/{mac}/config {"long_after_min": 1440, "long_poll_s": 3600}`: after a day without the AP a label checks only once an hour (the default). `long_after_min` 65535 turns it off. |
| Webhook for health reports | `esl_client.py config --webhook http://host/path` ([Health and webhook](Health-and-webhook)) |
| Radio transmit power | the dashboard's Settings page (Radio), `esl_client.py config --tx-power 8`, or `PUT /api/config {"tx_power": 8}`. 10 dBm by default; choose up to 20. |
| Radio channel | the dashboard's Settings page (Radio), `esl_client.py config --channel 20`, or `PUT /api/config {"channel": 20}`. 25 by default. The labels move with the AP. |
| Labels of other APs | the dashboard's Settings page (Radio), `esl_client.py config --adopt 1A2B3C4D` (`all` for any AP, `null` = off), or `PUT /api/config {"adopt": "1A2B3C4D"}`. Takes over labels of that AP once they have been without it for an hour, for 14 days. AP firmware 0.11.0 or newer ([Why labels stay connected](Why-labels-stay-connected#more-than-one-ap)). |
| WiFi network | the [Set up](https://mathiasbk.github.io/openESL/#setup) tab, the setup network, or `esl_client.py wifi "Network" --password ...` ([WiFi and API token](WiFi-and-API-token)) |
| API token | `esl_client.py --serial /dev/ttyACM0 wifi --new-token NEWTOKEN` |
| Name on the network | `esl_client.py wifi --hostname NAME` (gives `NAME.local`; `openesl` by default) |
| The AP's clock | set from the internet (NTP) once it is on WiFi; `esl_client.py time --set` sets it from your computer |
| Time zone | `esl_client.py config --tz "CET-1CEST,M3.5.0,M10.5.0/3"` (Norway), or `PUT /api/config {"tz": ...}`. A POSIX TZ string; UTC until set. Used for dates and times the AP fills in itself; `at` stays in UTC. |
| Dates on labels | `esl_client.py config --lang nb`, or `PUT /api/config {"lang": "nb"}`. The language of the date fields `{weekday}` and `{date}`: `en` (Thursday, 29 October 2026) or `nb` (Torsdag, 29. oktober 2026). The Dashboard's *Send text* uses it too. Until one is chosen the AP writes English, and *Send text* writes in the browser's language, as before. Labels with a date field are drawn again in the new language at their next poll. AP firmware 0.11.0 or newer. |
| Night mode | `esl_client.py config --night 22:00-06:00/1800`, or `PUT /api/config {"night": {"from": "22:00", "to": "06:00", "poll_s": 1800}}`, or the Dashboard's Settings, **Hours**. In the AP's time zone: labels check at most every `poll_s` (60 to 3600 s) at night. `off` / `null` ends it. AP firmware 0.11.0 or newer; nothing to update on the labels. |
| Update window | `esl_client.py config --update-window 06:00-08:00`, or `PUT /api/config {"update_window": {"from": "06:00", "to": "08:00"}}`, or Settings, **Hours**. New content goes out only in this daily window. `off` / `null` ends it. AP firmware 0.11.0 or newer. |
| NTP server | `esl_client.py config --ntp no.pool.ntp.org`; `""` goes back to the one built in (`pool.ntp.org`) |

**About the poll interval:** the labels ask the AP for news every 60 seconds by default. A longer interval saves battery, but a change takes longer to show. Do not shorten it to make one label faster: it costs battery all the time. When the AP is off or gone, a label checks every 10 minutes at most, and after a day only once an hour, so a label on a shelf without its AP lasts longer; when the AP comes back, such a label can take up to an hour to show up again.

**About the transmit power:** for the labels' radio (802.15.4), the EU allows about 13 dBm, counting the antenna's gain (EN 300 328 allows 20 dBm in total, but at most 10 dBm per MHz, and the signal is about 2 MHz wide). The AP sends at 10 dBm unless you set another value. With the chip or PCB antenna of a small board, up to 13 dBm keeps within the EU limit. With an external antenna of G dBi, the limit is about 13 − G (8 dBm for a 5 dBi antenna); the antenna still helps fully when the AP listens to the labels. Outside the EU, where more may be allowed, up to 20 dBm can be set. More power reaches labels further away, but their answers do not get any stronger: for those, a better antenna on the AP, placed high with a clear view of the labels, is what helps.

**About night mode and the update window:** both follow the AP's clock in its time zone (Settings, General), and neither applies while the AP does not know the time (after a restart, before the time server answers). In night mode a label that polls at night is told to come back after the night interval instead of its own, but only if it then wakes before the night ends; otherwise it gets its own interval. So every label is back on its own interval by morning, as spread out as before, and the AP sees no rush of labels at the end of the night. A label whose own interval is longer keeps it, and content scheduled for the night ([Scheduled prices](Scheduled-prices)) still shows on time. With 300 s by day and 1800 s from 22:00 to 06:00, a label polls about a third as often over a day. With an update window, a new price set outside it waits: the label keeps what it shows (the Dashboard says *pending*) and gets the new content at its first poll inside the window. A label that shows nothing from the AP yet (new, or after a reset) gets its first content at once, and settings and label firmware are not held. A window inside the night works too: labels go back to their own interval before it opens. Keep the window longer than the labels' poll interval, so every label polls inside it (the Dashboard warns when it is shorter). If the AP restarts during the night, a label that was told the night interval may show as late for the last hour of the night: it comes back on its own, nothing is lost.

**About the channel:** the labels and the AP talk on one 802.15.4 channel, 25 unless you choose another. Channel 25 sits above WiFi channel 11 and is clear of most WiFi in Europe; 15, 20 and 26 are the others clear of WiFi channels 1, 6 and 11. Change it if another device is busy on 25 (a Zigbee network, say). When you save, the AP tells each label at its next poll and moves once they all have it, at the latest after two of the longest poll intervals, so no label is left behind; **Move now** skips the wait. A label with firmware 0.9.0 or newer that has not heard of the new channel finds the AP again by itself: after three missed polls it looks on the built-in channel 25 and on 15, 20, 26 and 11, and then on one more channel each time it misses the AP, through all of 11–26. Update every label (and a radio on WiFi) to 0.9.0 first: older firmware cannot follow a channel change, so the AP refuses to move while it hears such a label, and the Settings page says how many there are. Several APs on one site can each have their own channel; from 0.11.0 a label with 0.11.0 firmware stays with its own AP while it looks for it ([Why labels stay connected](Why-labels-stay-connected#more-than-one-ap)).

## When building the firmware

`idf.py menuconfig` → "OpenESL AP": radio channel (the built-in one, 25), PAN ID, TX power (the built-in value, 10 dBm), the most labels to keep, the dashboard, OTA, the screen, and the API over USB. See [Building from source](Building-from-source) and [ap/README.md](https://github.com/mathiasbk/openESL/blob/master/ap/README.md#configuration).
